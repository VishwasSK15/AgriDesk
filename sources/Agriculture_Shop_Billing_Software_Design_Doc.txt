AGRICULTURE SHOP BILLING SOFTWARE
Design System & UI Direction
Reference-led aesthetic • Minimal • Practical • Offline-first
1. Design Direction
The interface should take the strongest qualities from the supplied invoicing references: a structured business dashboard, compact navigation, clear tables, restrained cards, strong primary actions, and highly readable invoice documents. The agriculture context should be introduced through a restrained green accent rather than a colorful “farm” visual style.
Minimalistic and professional — the UI should feel like business software, not a marketing website.
Fast to understand — important numbers, stock states, billing actions and payment status should be visible immediately.
Low visual noise — avoid gradients, excessive illustrations, glassmorphism and large decorative elements.
Practical first — optimize for a shop counter/laptop workflow where the operator performs repeated billing and stock tasks.
Consistent — the same spacing, typography, buttons, tables and status treatments should appear throughout the app.
2. Reference Analysis
Across the references, the recurring design language is:
Deep navy/blue is used for the application chrome, page headers and primary actions.
White/light-gray surfaces create a clean workspace with strong content separation.
Pale lavender/blue table and card backgrounds are used sparingly to soften dense business screens.
Green is reserved for successful/paid states and positive actions; red is reserved for stock alerts, overdue invoices and destructive actions.
Rounded rectangles are common, but the radius remains moderate rather than highly playful.
Tables are dense but readable, with clear column hierarchy and compact row actions.
Icons are simple line icons with small colored containers.
Typography is modern sans-serif, medium/bold for headings and regular for supporting information.
Primary workflows use obvious filled buttons while secondary actions remain outlined or icon-only.
Large white space is used around major sections so the interface does not feel crowded.

4. Theme Strategy
Support two themes from the start:
Light theme: default business environment — white surfaces, very light gray workspace, navy navigation/header and muted green accents.
Dark theme: same structure and hierarchy, with charcoal surfaces and lighter text. Do not simply invert every color.
Theme switching should preserve semantic colors: green = positive, amber = warning, red = critical.
Avoid pure black (#000) and pure white everywhere; use softened neutrals for comfortable long sessions.

6. Application Layout
Desktop-first shell: fixed left sidebar + top page header + scrollable content workspace.
Sidebar width: approximately 220–240 px.
Top header: approximately 52–60 px; keep it visually quiet.
Main content max width: approximately 1200–1400 px, centered when the screen is wide.
Use a consistent 8 px spacing system: 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 px.
Cards should normally use 10–12 px radius and a subtle 1 px border; use shadows only where elevation is needed.
Avoid nested cards wherever possible.

8. Dashboard Design
Compact welcome/header strip with current date and shop context.
Four to six metric cards maximum: Today's Sales, Bills Today, Outstanding, Total Products, Low Stock, optionally Customers.
Use small icon containers rather than large illustrations.
Inventory alerts should appear below the metrics: Out of Stock and Low Stock.
Sales summary should use a simple chart with minimal gridlines and one primary data color.
Inventory summary should show top low-stock products and fast-moving items.
Insights should be written as short sentences, e.g. “Fertilizer sales increased this week” rather than filling the screen with charts.
9. Billing / New Bill Screen
Make this the fastest screen in the application.
Customer selection at the top, with a simple “Walk-in Customer” option.
Product search should be prominent and keyboard-friendly.
Selected items appear in a clean table: Item, Qty, Rate, Discount, Tax, Amount, Remove.
Show stock availability immediately when a product is selected.
Keep totals in a visually distinct but simple right-side or bottom summary panel.
Primary action: Save & Print Bill. Secondary action: Save Bill / Save Draft.
Printing is a mandatory workflow: after successful billing, show Print Bill clearly and keep it available from invoice history.
10. Invoice / Bill Document
Use a white document surface even inside the application.
Shop logo/name at top left; invoice number/date at top right.
Seller and customer blocks below the header.
Item table with strong column labels and restrained borders.
Subtotal, discount, tax and grand total aligned on the right.
Payment information/UPI QR can sit below the totals when configured.
Keep the printed bill black/gray with the primary blue used only for small branding elements so it remains economical and readable when printed.
The print preview should closely match the final printed/PDF output.
11. Inventory & Product Management
Table-first layout similar to the reference invoice/customer screens.
Columns: Product, Category, Unit, HSN/SKU, Purchase Rate, Selling Rate, Stock, Reorder Level, Status.
Use compact status badges: In Stock, Low Stock, Out of Stock.
Product creation/editing should use a simple two-column form on desktop and one column on smaller screens.
For agricultural products, allow fields such as batch number, expiry date, manufacturer/brand and applicable tax where required by the PRD.
Never use red as a general product color; reserve it for stock-critical states.
12. Customer & Supplier Management
Follow the reference customer-management pattern: add form on the left, searchable table on the right.
Search should be visible without opening a filter panel.
Use pagination for large datasets.
Row actions should be icon buttons with tooltips: View, Edit, Delete.
Supplier records should support purchase/inventory workflows without introducing a separate visual language.
13. Reports & Sales Insights
Keep analytics simple and operational rather than dashboard-heavy.
Core views: Sales Summary, Product Sales, Profit/Margin Summary if supported by the data, Outstanding Payments, Inventory Movement.
Date filters should be easy: Today, This Week, This Month, Custom.
Use one main chart at a time with supporting numeric cards.
AI/summary-style insights, if included, should appear as short neutral observations with the underlying period clearly shown.
Inventory insights should highlight low stock, dead/slow-moving stock and fast-moving products where data supports it.
14. Component Style
Buttons: 8–10 px radius, medium weight, clear labels. Primary = navy; positive inventory action = muted green.
Inputs: 40–44 px height, light border, clear focus ring, subtle background.
Tables: 44–52 px row height, light separators, sticky header when useful.
Badges: small, lightly tinted background with matching text; avoid saturated fills.
Dialogs: centered, white/dark surface, 12–16 px radius, clear title and primary action.
Toasts: compact bottom/right notification with semantic color.
Icon buttons: 32–36 px square, simple line icons, tooltip on hover.
Empty states: short explanation + one primary action; avoid illustrations unless necessary.
15. Iconography
Use one consistent line-icon library such as Lucide.
Prefer familiar symbols: receipt, package, warehouse/box, users, truck, chart, settings, printer.
Agriculture-specific icons should be used sparingly — leaf, seed, sprout or field icons can identify categories, not decorate every screen.
Icon stroke weight should remain consistent.
16. Responsive Behaviour
Desktop: persistent sidebar and full tables.
Tablet: narrower sidebar or collapsible sidebar; tables may become horizontally scrollable.
Mobile: sidebar becomes a drawer; metric cards become a 2-column grid; billing totals remain easy to reach.
Do not shrink dense tables until they become unreadable. Prefer horizontal scrolling or a mobile row/card representation.
Touch targets should be at least about 40–44 px.
17. What to Avoid
Do not use multiple bright accent colors just to make screens look lively.
Do not use heavy gradients, glassmorphism or oversized shadows.
Do not turn every metric into a colorful card.
Do not hide primary billing actions inside menus.
Do not overload the dashboard with charts.
Do not use decorative agricultural imagery in operational screens.
Do not create different button styles for every page.
18. Overall UI Recipe
Think: “modern offline accounting software with a subtle agricultural identity.” The visual hierarchy should be approximately 80% neutral surfaces and typography, 15% navy/blue brand UI, and 5% semantic green/amber/red states. The result should feel calm, reliable and quick to operate.

20. Final Design Principle
Simple enough for daily billing. Detailed enough for business control.
Every screen should answer three questions quickly: What is happening? What needs attention? What can I do next? If a visual element does not help answer one of these questions, it should probably be removed.
