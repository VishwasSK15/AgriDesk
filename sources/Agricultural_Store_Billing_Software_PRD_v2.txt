Agricultural Store Billing & Inventory Management System
Updated Product Requirements Document (PRD) — Version 2.0
Update highlights: Mandatory bill printing has been promoted to a core acceptance requirement. A new Sales Insights & Inventory Insights capability has been added to summarize business data and surface actionable, explainable observations.
Target Platform: Desktop-first application with offline-first operation
Primary User: Agricultural Store / Fertilizer & Pesticide Shop Owner
Target Market: Agricultural input retailers in India
1. Product Overview
The Agricultural Store Billing & Inventory Management System is an offline-first billing, customer management, inventory, GST invoicing, purchasing, credit management and business-insights application designed specifically for agricultural product retailers.
Maintain customer records locally.
Create configurable GST invoices.
Print a physical bill/receipt as a mandatory part of the completed-sale workflow.
Search previous transactions by customer, mobile number, invoice, product and date.
Track product stock automatically after sales, purchases and returns.
Track batch, manufacturing date and expiry information.
Monitor low-stock, out-of-stock and expiring products.
Manage customer credit, payments and outstanding balances.
Send payment reminders through supported SMS/WhatsApp integrations.
Continue core billing operations without internet connectivity.
Generate reports and summarize sales/inventory data into understandable business insights.
Maintain supplier and purchase records.
Provide explainable recommendations based on recorded business data rather than unsupported assumptions.
2. Product Vision
Build a practical agricultural retail operating system—not merely a billing application. The product should make counter billing fast, keep inventory reliable, preserve customer history, and help the shop owner understand what is happening in the business.
Core principle: Fast enough for a busy billing counter, simple enough for a small shop owner, reliable enough for offline operation, and intelligent enough to summarize the shop's own sales and inventory data.
3. Problem Statement
Customer records are often maintained manually and are difficult to retrieve.
Manual billing can be slow and error-prone.
A bill may be generated but not consistently printed or handed to the customer.
Inventory quantities can become inaccurate when sales and purchases are tracked separately.
Agricultural products require useful batch and expiry visibility.
Credit sales and outstanding balances are difficult to monitor.
Internet connectivity can interrupt counter operations.
Raw sales reports do not always tell a shop owner what changed or what deserves attention.
Inventory reports show quantities but may not clearly explain fast-moving, slow-moving, dead-stock or replenishment patterns.

9. Mandatory Bill Printing — Core Requirement
Bill printing is a mandatory product feature, not an optional convenience feature.
Mandatory Completion Rule
A sale may be saved as a draft/held bill without printing. However, a finalized sale must enter the Print Bill step. The normal successful-sale workflow is: Finalize Sale → Generate Invoice → Print Bill → Complete Transaction.
Supported Outputs
58mm thermal receipt
80mm thermal receipt
A4 GST invoice
PDF copy as a supplementary digital record
Printing Requirements
Print button must be clearly visible on the final billing screen.
Provide Save & Print / Finalize & Print as the primary completion action.
Allow reprinting from Sales History.
Allow selection/configuration of the default printer.
Support print preview where appropriate.
Handle printer unavailable/offline conditions with a clear recovery path.
A local copy of the invoice must remain available even if printing fails.
The system must distinguish 'invoice generated' from 'bill successfully printed' in the transaction/audit state where technically feasible.
Recommended Print Flow
User completes the cart.
System validates stock, pricing, customer and tax configuration.
User finalizes the sale.
System creates the invoice transaction.
System opens the print workflow automatically.
Bill is sent to the configured printer.
System records the print attempt/result.
User can reprint the invoice later from Sales History.
Important Edge Case
If the printer is unavailable, the sale should not be silently lost. The application should preserve the finalized invoice and clearly show that printing is pending/failed, with an immediate Retry Print action. The business owner can configure whether a transaction is allowed to remain finalized after a print failure based on operational needs.

25. Insight Engine Requirements
Insights should be based on deterministic calculations over the store's recorded data. The system should clearly distinguish facts, calculated comparisons and suggestions.
Insight Type
Example Logic
Sales change
Compare current period with previous comparable period.
Top product
Rank products by configured metric such as quantity or sales value.
Average bill
Total sales ÷ number of finalized sales.
Low stock
Current quantity below configured reorder level.
Fast moving
High sales velocity over a configured lookback period.
Slow moving
Low or zero movement over a configured lookback period.
Expiry exposure
Stock associated with batches inside selected expiry window.
Reorder suggestion
Historical movement + current stock + configured lead time/safety stock, where sufficient data exists.
The insight engine must avoid presenting speculation as fact. When data is insufficient, it should say so—for example, 'Not enough historical sales data to identify a reliable trend.'

43. Critical Business Rules
A finalized sale must enter the mandatory bill-print workflow.
A finalized sale must automatically update stock.
A cancelled sale must create appropriate reversal/adjustment records.
Every stock modification must have a reason/reference.
Expired products must be clearly identified and should not be silently sold.
Invoice numbers must remain unique.
Financial transactions should be reversed/cancelled rather than permanently deleted.
Customer outstanding must be derived from the transaction ledger.
Inventory must be auditable through stock movements or an equivalent mechanism.
Offline transactions must have unique transaction IDs.
Tax configuration must be editable/auditable rather than permanently hard-coded.
Sales and inventory insights must be calculated from stored business data.
Insights must distinguish measured facts from calculated comparisons and recommendations.
The system must not claim a trend or recommendation when there is insufficient data.
44. Important Edge Cases
Printer unavailable after finalization: preserve invoice, mark print pending/failed, show Retry Print.
Paper out or printer error: show actionable printer error without losing transaction.
Out of stock: block sale unless authorized override is enabled.
Insufficient stock: clearly show requested vs available.
Expired batch: block or require authorized override based on policy.
Customer without mobile: allow if permitted.
Walk-in customer: provide generic customer profile.
Internet failure: continue local billing and printing.
Power failure: transactional writes and recovery mechanisms minimize data loss.
Insufficient sales history: show 'not enough data' rather than inventing trends.
Inventory with no movement history: avoid labeling it as slow-moving until the configured observation window is met.

48. Final Product Vision
The final product should combine POS billing, agricultural inventory management, customer credit management, mandatory physical bill printing, and understandable business intelligence in one offline-first application.
The owner should be able to answer five questions quickly:
What did I sell today?
Who bought what and what do they still owe?
What stock do I have right now?
What stock needs attention because it is low, slow-moving or expiring?
What changed in my business recently, and what does the recorded data suggest I should look at?
The system should answer these questions using clear figures, concise summaries and traceable calculations—not unexplained or speculative conclusions.
